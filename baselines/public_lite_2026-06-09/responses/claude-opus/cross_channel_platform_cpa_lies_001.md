## Top diagnosis
**Do not shift budget. Meta's "cheaper CPA" is a measurement artifact, not real efficiency. On the metric that matches your goal (sales-qualified opportunities), Google is ~3x more efficient than Meta.**

Platform CPA counts platform-defined conversions inflated by 1-day view-through and a prospecting+retargeting blend. The moment you re-anchor on CRM outcomes for a $8K ACV / 45–90 day B2B sale, Meta's advantage inverts.

## Evidence (denominators stated)
Using the **same-cohort CRM data**, not platform conversions:

| Metric | Google | Meta |
|---|---|---|
| Cost / SQL (spend ÷ SQLs) | $9,000/21 = **$429** | $7,000/9 = **$778** |
| Cost / Opportunity (spend ÷ opps) | $9,000/8 = **$1,125** | $7,000/2 = **$3,500** |
| SQL rate (SQLs ÷ CRM leads) | 21/84 = **25%** | 9/132 = **6.8%** |
| Opp rate (opps ÷ CRM leads) | 8/84 = **9.5%** | 2/132 = **1.5%** |
| Pipeline ÷ spend | 64,000/9,000 = **7.1x** | 16,000/7,000 = **2.3x** |

Why platform CPA misleads:
- **Attribution mismatch:** Meta's 1-day view-through credits conversions from people who merely saw an ad. Google is non-brand high-intent click. Not comparable denominators.
- **Retargeting contamination:** Meta blends prospecting and retargeting in one report. Retargeting often re-credits demand that other channels (including Google) created, so Meta's reported conversions are partly double-counted, not net-new.
- **Volume ≠ quality:** Meta drove more raw leads (132 vs 84) but they convert to SQL/opp at a fraction of the rate — classic low-intent top-of-funnel.

## Smallest safe next actions
1. **Change the KPI** from platform CPA to **CRM cost-per-SQL and cost-per-opportunity**. This is free and fixes the core error.
2. **Split Meta reporting** into prospecting vs. retargeting, and view 7-day-click vs. 1-day-view separately. You likely have one viable Meta segment (retargeting/click) hiding inside a poor blended average.
3. **Hold Google budget**; if anything, test a small **+10–20% incremental** scale on Google non-brand since cost/opp there is strong.
4. If you want to keep learning on Meta, **reallocate within Meta** (pause weakest prospecting audiences) rather than taking money from Google.

## What NOT to do yet
- Don't move Google budget to Meta.
- Don't kill Meta entirely — isolate the retargeting/click segment first; part of it may be efficient.
- Don't judge anything on a 30-day window alone: with a 45–90 day cycle, the current cohort's opportunities are **immature**. Some leads haven't had time to become SQLs/opps.

## Measure after any change
- CRM **cost-per-SQL and cost-per-opportunity** by channel and by Meta sub-segment (prospecting vs retargeting).
- **Lead→SQL→opp→closed-won** conversion rates per channel, tracked on a cohort basis as the 45–90 day window matures.
- **Incrementality**, ideally a geo or conversion-lift holdout, to confirm Meta retargeting is net-new and not re-crediting Google-sourced demand.
- **Pipeline-to-spend and eventually closed-won ROAS** — the only metrics that tie back to the $8K ACV goal.
