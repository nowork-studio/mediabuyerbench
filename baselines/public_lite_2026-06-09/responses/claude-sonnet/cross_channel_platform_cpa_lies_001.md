## Top Diagnosis: Meta's cheaper platform CPA is a vanity metric driven by attribution inflation and audience mixing.

When you trace leads through to the metrics that actually matter — SQLs and opportunities — Google is the cheaper channel by a wide margin.

---

## Evidence

**Cost per SQL (denominator: CRM-verified SQLs)**
| Channel | Spend | SQLs | Cost/SQL | SQL Rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Google | $9,000 | 21 | **$428** | 21/84 = 25% |
| Meta | $7,000 | 9 | **$778** | 9/132 = 6.8% |

Meta costs **82% more per SQL** despite a cheaper platform CPA.

**Cost per opportunity (denominator: CRM opportunities)**
| Channel | Spend | Opps | Cost/Opp | Pipeline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Google | $9,000 | 8 | **$1,125** | $64,000 |
| Meta | $7,000 | 2 | **$3,500** | $16,000 |

Meta costs **3.1x more per opportunity** and generates **4x less pipeline per dollar spent**.

**Pipeline efficiency**
- Google: $7.11 pipeline per $1 spent
- Meta: $2.29 pipeline per $1 spent

---

## Why Meta's Platform CPA Looks Cheaper

Three compounding distortions:

1. **View-through attribution**: Meta counts 1-day view conversions — people who saw an ad but never clicked. These inflate conversion volume without intent signal. Google's conversions are predominantly click-based from high-intent non-brand queries.

2. **Prospecting + retargeting mixed in same report**: Retargeting converts cheaper and at higher SQL rates. Bundling them hides that prospecting CPAs may be far worse than $50.

3. **Platform CPA denominator is form fills, not qualified pipeline**: 140 Meta "conversions" produced 2 opportunities. The conversion definition is doing a lot of heavy lifting.

---

## Recommended Next Actions (smallest safe steps)

1. **Do not shift budget from Google to Meta.** Google is generating $7.11 in pipeline per dollar at a 25% SQL rate. That is performing well for B2B SaaS with an $8k ACV.

2. **Segment Meta reporting immediately.** Split prospecting vs. retargeting into separate campaigns with separate conversion tracking. You likely have a retargeting tail subsidizing terrible prospecting economics.

3. **Switch Meta attribution to click-only (7-day click, 0-day view) in your reporting view.** Compare reported conversions to CRM leads again after stripping view-through — this will reveal true prospecting CPA.

4. **If you test Meta budget increases, do it as a controlled experiment**: +$1,500–2,000 incrementally with prospecting only, click-only attribution, tracked to SQL within 30 days in CRM before any broader reallocation.

---

## What Not to Do Yet

- **Do not pause Meta entirely.** Retargeting likely has legitimate value at low spend; you'd lose bottom-of-funnel coverage.
- **Do not optimize Meta toward platform CPA.** That signal is corrupted. Optimize toward SQL rate once segments are clean.
- **Do not increase Google budget reflexively without checking capacity.** Confirm the sales team is working the 21 SQLs properly before flooding more leads in — 8/21 SQLs becoming opportunities (38%) is reasonable but worth pressure-testing.

---

## What to Measure After Changes

| Metric | Timeframe | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Meta prospecting cost/SQL (click-only) | 30 days | Benchmark vs. current $778 |
| Meta retargeting SQL rate separately | 30 days | Expect significantly higher than 6.8% |
| Google SQL rate stability | Ongoing | Watch for quality decay if spend increases |
| Opportunity-to-close rate by channel | 90 days | With a 45–90 day sales cycle, current opps aren't closed yet — this is the final arbiter |

The last point matters most: you have 8 Google opportunities worth $64k and 2 Meta opportunities worth $16k in pipeline, but none have closed yet in this cohort. Close rates by channel — tracked in CRM once this cohort completes — will be the definitive signal for long-term budget allocation.
